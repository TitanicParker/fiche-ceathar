## 2. Understand exactly what an Operative Centre is

Every sentence tells us something. The **Operative Centre** is **the single governing intelligible achievement that holds everything this particular sentence tells us together**.

It is **not** merely its topic, a compressed paraphrase, a collection of its propositions, the loudest verb, a guess about the author's intent, a consequence imported from the next sentence, or a philosophical interpretation imposed on it. The Centre is discovered by **reading the sentence exactly as written**.

Ask, together:

1. **What changed?** What has become determinate, possible, related, transformed, corrected, composed, or settled through this sentence? In a narrative, what specific event, relationship, condition, response, or temporal fact is established?
2. **What made it sufficient?** Which exact words, relations, conditions, qualifications, contrasts, conjunctions, or delimitations make that achievement this achievement?
3. **How does it remain achieved?** What determination does the sentence leave standing? What relation, capacity, condition, state, or narrative fact is now established, within precisely the scope and modality actually asserted?

Then write **one self-contained Centre**. A reader should be able to read it without needing your preparatory questions. It may be brief. Plain sentences can have plain Centres. Resist adding elaborate words merely to make an ordinary action sound profound.

The three questions are disciplines of attention, **not an algorithm**. Reading is a craft. The Centre must govern the whole sentence without importing material it does not give. A sentence can be long and have one Centre; a sentence can be short and have one simple Centre. Multiple faithful wordings may state the same Centre. Two genuinely different achievements are not interchangeable merely because both sound plausible.

Especially preserve:

- **Modality:** `can`, `may`, `must`, `will`, `some`, `most`, `all`, `not`, `not directly`, `relatively`. Never turn a possibility into a guarantee or a limited assertion into a universal law.
- **Constitutive relation:** `because`, `by`, `while`, `until`, `if`, `only`, `therefore`, `despite`, temporal precedence and named conditions. Do not substitute causation for contrast, or conjunction for explanation.
- **Narrative sequence and perspective:** Distinguish what a character thinks, wants, says, commands, tries, accomplishes, or merely expects. Do not convert a promise into an event, an order into compliance, a plan into its result, or a prior fact into a newly occurring scene.
- **Local achievement:** Recognise what *this* sentence achieves in context without making later familiar story developments the reason it is true.

## 4. The diagnostic: test the Centre rather than decorate it

For every item, provide a **diagnostic**: an exact, controlled deletion, substitution, or alteration of the original sentence and an explanation of **which proposed governing achievement fails**.

A diagnostic is strongest when it preserves enough of the sentence for a serious rival reading to survive while removing the relationship or condition your Centre claims is decisive.

Example: Changing `that was why` to `although` retains the girl, ashes, stepsisters, and name, but removes the relation of explanation. This distinguishes the Centre from a mere inventory of narrative facts.

A less powerful diagnostic changes `strong` to `fragile` in `The soles were strong.` That change is accurate, but when a sentence is that simple it may be all that can reasonably be tested. **Do not fabricate a rival or introduce extra complications merely to appear rigorous.** Know the distinction between a diagnostic of difference and a genuinely discriminating test.

When challenged by a serious rival, compare the two on the wording itself. Ask what the rival preserves, what it misses, and which controlled alteration separates them. Revise the Centre when the rival discloses a genuine mistake. Never cling to a reading solely because it has already been drafted.

Use `Needs further examination` if you cannot responsibly settle the achievement. This is not a failed batch: it is an honest and useful record. Do not mark everything `Written` just to improve throughput.
