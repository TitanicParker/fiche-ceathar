## 3. What counts as a faithful reading

An Operative Centre is **the singular governing intelligible achievement holding together everything a particular sentence tells us**. It is not the grammatical topic, a decorative paraphrase, a loose summary, a predicted consequence, an imposed theory or what a familiar story will later reveal.

The worker must ask:

1. **What changed?**
2. **What made it sufficient?**
3. **How does it remain achieved?**

The record must preserve `can`, `may`, `some`, `most`, `must`, negation, conditions, relative qualifications, temporal ordering, who asserts what, and whether a character merely wishes, promises, commands, attempts, perceives or accomplishes something.

Diagnostic alterations must test the claimed Centre, not simply show that the sentence changes if a prominent word changes. In a simple sentence a simple diagnostic may be all that is available; do not demand artificial ingenuity. In a compound or contested sentence, a serious rival should be confronted by a discriminating test.

**Three useful examples from the existing record:**

- **A-170, correlation:** Correlation alone does *not by itself* establish causation in either direction. The Centre cannot say causation is impossible.
- **A-197, lossless compression:** Fewer bits *while* allowing **exact reconstruction** is one jointly achieved determination; neither feature is enough alone.
- **207, Cinderella:** Her habitual position among hearth ashes is given as the **reason** for her nickname. Changing `that was why` to `although` preserves many facts but destroys that explanation.

Read existing entries and the Notebook when judging demanding cases. Avoid repetitive, trivial nitpicking; identify substantive failures and accept precise alternative phrasings of the same Centre.

## The reading craft

The **Operative Centre** is the **single governing intelligible achievement holding together everything this sentence tells us**, not merely its topic, paraphrase, most conspicuous verb, imagined consequence, or what its story eventually reveals.

Read the exact sentence and ask:

1. **What changed?**
2. **What made it sufficient?**
3. **How does it remain achieved?**

Compose one precise, self-contained Centre. Retain actual conditions, quantifiers, negations, modality, cause versus contrast, temporal order, points of view, and the difference between wanting, trying, ordering, reporting and achieving. Do not add knowledge from later passages. Plain sentences may have plain Centres.

Then write a **diagnostic**: a controlled alteration of the original sentence that removes the governing achievement. Explain why. For hard cases, test a serious rival reading. A diagnostic should expose the relation or condition that holds the sentence together, not merely change an arbitrary word. When genuinely unsettled, mark `Needs further examination`, not `Written`.

Read representative completed records such as **A-170** (correlation does not *by itself* establish causation), **A-197** (fewer bits *with exact reconstruction*), **207** (hearth ashes expressly explain Cinderella's name), **336** (absence contrasted with continuing remembrance), and **382** (growth *until* concealment).
