### Sentence Craft — one method, three synchronized expressions

From this point forward, the **research corpus, the public Notebook and the Sentence Craft game advance in step under the same Centre-first method**. These are not three independent projects, nor three authorities allowed to contradict one another.

**1. Research record — the source of the reading.** Begin with the next exact canonical sentence and its stable ID. Read it before selecting any 54-cell coordinate. Record *What changed? What made it sufficient? How does it remain achieved?* State the self-contained Operative Centre and a diagnostic alteration that would destroy that achievement. Preserve the original wording, status and any unresolved alternatives. The current written Centre record is [Resolve — Operative Centres](https://github.com/TitanicParker/resolve/blob/main/OPERATIVE_CENTRES.md); verify its live progress register before claiming a sentence has been completed. A written editorial Centre is not automatically a frozen confirmed Centre or a canonical cell classification.

**2. Notebook — the teaching of the reading.** As readings are earned, develop them into [Notebook instalments](../notebook.html) that show the art of recognition: the decisive wording, the governing relation, a serious rival reading, the diagnostic and what the encounter teaches. The Notebook need not publish one essay for every single source sentence, but it must not outrun the underlying research record or quietly alter its Centre. New advances in the method itself should also be explained here.

**3. Sentence Craft — the practice of discrimination.** Bring those same verified readings into the [Sentence Craft game](../sentence-craft.html). Each question preserves the source sentence and recorded Centre, and supplies three *developed, plausible, argumentative* rival readings. Do not make the correct option identifiable by its greater length, more formal register, or obvious detail; match the alternatives for seriousness and approximate development. Each rival should fail for a *specific* reason: a changed modality, displaced condition, substituted consequence, imported explanation, mistaken governing relation, or other identifiable difference. Randomize positions. After selection, reveal the exact recorded Centre and the discriminating diagnostic.

**The governing rule:** The Centre is not everything the sentence tells us; it is what holds everything the sentence tells us together. There are **no near misses** in the identity of the Centre: a choice identifies that achievement or identifies something else. Distinct phrasings can express the same Centre, and readers may disagree or revise their judgement. The game gives no partial credit for a different achievement, but its explanations should make every mistake educative. The three questions are disciplines of attention; the diagnostic is a discipline of discrimination. Neither is an algorithm that replaces reading.

**Release sequence:** canonical source → source-faithful written Centre and diagnostic → Notebook teaching treatment and/or game question → cross-check of wording, answer, rival arguments and links → publication. For each new batch, update the corpus progress truthfully, add appropriate teaching material, expand or refine the game, and verify that all three remain consistent. A game level may collect several readings by the skill it trains; level order is pedagogical, not a substitute for canonical source order. If a reading changes substantively, record the revision in the research lineage and review its Notebook and game appearances together.

**Present state (7 November 2026):** The Notebook has 31 instalments, including [Sentence Craft — The Art of Reading](../notebook.html#installation-31); the game has six five-question levels drawn from 30 selected written readings, with 90 developed rival options. These are the current teaching selections, **not** a claim that all written Centres have been converted into game questions. Check live files for later progress.

This is an **apprenticeship in reading**: the corpus supplies the material, the Notebook demonstrates workmanship, and the game exercises the discrimination that workmanship requires. None may silently substitute a plausible paraphrase for the governing achievement.

## THE SOCRATIC METHOD FOR THIS STAGE — Microscope and reciprocal microscope

**The inquiry is cumulative, not perpetually restarted.** The reader is not asked to doubt the existence of every previously earned distinction at the beginning of every new chapter. A recognition established by a valid argument or a decisive ordinary encounter should become a premise for investigating something further. Philosophical progress can consist in making explicit what those premises already entail.

**Microscope — inward examination.** Take an established fact or distinction and look more closely into its constitution: *Precisely what has been established? What is its scope? What conditions are indispensable? Which apparent sameness hides a difference?* This is not rhetorical scepticism; it is a way of obtaining greater resolution.

**Reciprocal microscope — outward examination.** Look **through** an established distinction to what it makes newly recognizable: *If that is so, what follows? In what further circumstance must this difference matter? What larger necessary relation is beginning to emerge?* The movement outward must preserve the premises' exact scope; it cannot promote a suggestive analogy into a universal claim.

**Working sequence:**

1. **Recall an earned fact** from a precise chapter passage or argument. State it in words the original encounter supports, not as a stronger proposition than was shown.
2. **Use it as a premise**, provisionally stable within its scope. Do not reopen it merely to fill a page with questions.
3. **Increase the resolution:** ask what the fact necessarily distinguishes, presupposes or excludes. Work through an ordinary encounter until the answer is recognizable.
4. **Reverse the lens:** ask what new fact, relation or implication is made accessible *because* the earlier distinction has been established. Derive it, rather than announcing it.
5. **Record the advance:** write the old premise, the new recognition, the bridge of reasoning, and the boundary of the conclusion. The new fact becomes available for the next chapter.
6. **Reopen only where needed:** if a counterexample defeats a premise or changes its scope, correct **that** premise and every dependent conclusion, preserving what remains valid. A hypothesis remains a hypothesis; the 54's proposed exhaustiveness is not automatically a proved fact.

### The living function of microscopic reciprocity — one moment at a time

**The microscope and reciprocal microscope are not merely two instructions for examining a finished account. They describe a recurrent function of intelligible inquiry itself.** We encounter something; we ask; an answer makes one aspect sufficiently determinate; that achievement changes what can now be asked. The inward microscope increases the resolution of what has been recognized. The outward microscope discovers what follows from that recognition. The new discovery becomes the starting condition for another inward examination, and the movement continues.

In ordinary life the interrogatives **what, when, who, where, why, and how** give familiar directions to inquiry. They are not a six-cell alternative to the 54, nor do they map one-to-one onto the six Transformation Patterns. A *why* question, for example, might be completed by making a relation intelligible, establishing a repaired condition, or disclosing how parts operate together. The interrogative identifies the demand; it does not predetermine the conjunctive form of its completion.

**An assertion creates further intelligible possibilities because a sentence does not stand without context.** “The door closed” can complete the question *what happened?* and make *when?*, *how?*, or *why?* newly relevant. Conversely, *why did it close?* governs what would count as an adequate answer: “at noon” might be true while still failing that demand. Not every conceivable follow-up is owed by the original assertion. Distinguish demands inherent in the asserted content, demands established by the live undertaking, and questions newly introduced by a later investigator.

The movement is therefore not a mechanical sequence in which every answer automatically settles the next question:

**Encounter → question → undertaking-relative sufficient determination → further available question → finer examination → further determination.**

The questions are historically situated, revisable, and often ephemeral. An achievement can nevertheless be sufficient *in its own respect* without settling every question about its subject. Further inquiry need not cancel what was earned. It may preserve it, qualify its scope, show a constitutive condition was missing, or change its bearing within a richer account.

**One sentence at a time. One realisation at a time. One moment at a time.** A question may become more exact even before its answer is available. Discovering which question must be asked can itself complete a distinct undertaking. But do not confuse a question's having become intelligible with the successful answer to that question.

This has a reflexive consequence: **the investigation of intelligibility participates in the activity it studies.** The guide is an instrument undergoing correction through the discoveries it helps bring into view. Its records should accumulate actual advances rather than continually restart the same debate. The next visitor should ask what a newly earned recognition makes possible that could not responsibly have been asked before.

**Relation to the Great Human Invariant.** The project proposes that the 54 identify the invariant, jointly constituted forms in which such particular intelligible achievements come to completion and remain available. The recurrence of question, answer and further question is a way of investigating that thesis; it does not by itself prove uniqueness or exhaustiveness of the 54. Do not classify questions by their interrogative words. Individuate the completed achievement, identify its live demand, and only then ask whether the controlling grammar specifies its invariant form.

**Additional microscope record fields:** What was the previous achieved determination? Which interrogative or implicit demand did it make possible? What new difference is actually established by the next answer? Which earlier result survives, is narrowed, or is overturned? What further question is now earned? Record when no further conclusion is warranted rather than manufacturing a new turn in the cycle.

### An example of cumulative Socratic movement

Socratia has already distinguished **understanding what a sentence claims** from **establishing that what it claims is true**. Do not exhaust another chapter asking again whether false assertions can be intelligible. Start from that established difference. Ask, for instance: **If two people understand contradictory claims, what is available between them before either has settled which claim is true?** Their common understanding of the *question at issue* may itself be examined for necessity, its conditions, and its limits. The inquiry could then look outward to what makes a genuine disagreement determinable—and inward to when differing undertakings mean that the speakers were never contradicting one another in the same respect. This is an illustration of **method**, not a compulsory Chapter XVIII plot or an asserted result.

### What counts as a fact here?

Distinguish **observed or constructed example**, **directly recognizable difference**, **conclusion argued from that difference**, and **architectural thesis still to be established**. An established claim becomes a working fact **within the scope justified by its evidence and reasoning**. This is how the investigation can accumulate knowledge without calling every appealing idea a fact or allowing every new GPT to erase what earlier chapters earned.

The manuscript's question-and-answer form is a **method of discovery**, not a ritual of uncertainty. Questions should be capable of yielding new, determinate answers. The reader should be able to say **what is now known that was not clear at the chapter's beginning**. One sound new recognition is better than a thousand questions that return to the same premise.

## Foundational orientation: the Great Human Invariant

**The universe changes; our descriptions and understanding change. The project proposes that the forms in which such things become intelligibly complete do not change.** This is the thesis provisionally called the **Great Human Invariant**. “Invariant” does not mean that things, experiences, meanings or historical knowledge are static. It means that a form of intelligible completion may preserve its identity across changes in expression and circumstance. The proposal reaches future as well as historical expressions: new subjects of understanding need not amount to new primitive kinds of intelligibility.

### The three-sentence encounter: Cell 23

Before formal classification, read these three sentences:

> **In a right-angled triangle, the square on the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares on the other two sides.**
>
> **As the temperature increased, the pressure increased.**
>
> **The longer the day went on, the sorer my feet became.**

The first asserts a mathematical equality under the conditions of Euclidean right-triangle geometry, a special case of the law of cosines. It is **not** a causal sequence in which the hypotenuse makes the other sides become what they are. The second reports a physical covariation in the described conditions; it does not assert an unrestricted gas law. The third reports a relationship between elapsed time and experienced soreness. The three claims have different domains, warrants and degrees of necessity. Yet they invite recognition of an intelligible relation constituted **across** terms, whose identity **bears through** that relationship.

The project's **specific structural thesis** is **Cell 23: Relating × Distributed × Bearing**. This is a **unified conjunctive identity**, not three adjectives appended independently. The relational achievement is intelligible in the mutual bearing of the relevant terms, not in either term alone. The controlling reference's canonical description and examples must be consulted before any detailed derivation.

The Pythagorean example supplies an unusually clear question about translation. The theorem can be stated in changing languages, notation and word orders while preserving **the same mathematically intelligible relation**. The project asserts that the invariant relational achievement, faithfully articulated, therefore retains **Cell 23** across those expressions. The spoken sentence, the mathematical relation, a particular proof, and the listener's recognition need **not** all be the same completion-event or have the same coordinate. A translation can also alter the linguistic action of an utterance while preserving its mathematical subject. This distinction protects the claim from both careless overextension and evasive reinterpretation.

**Investigate the claim of necessity in its intended scope.** Do not reduce it to “classifiers tend to choose Cell 23”; do not replace it with “every sentence mentioning Pythagoras is Cell 23.” Ask what faithful preservation of *the same relational intelligibility* entails, and whether there is a principled counterexample. A purported counterexample must state whether it changes the relation or merely its linguistic delivery.

The cross-domain comparison is a **recognition exercise**, not a proof that all physical, mathematical and experiential relations have the same truth conditions. Its task is to expose a candidate invariant in what these statements make intelligible.

### A further encounter: negation without a change of Cell 23

Now change precisely one word in the Pythagorean sentence:

> **Affirmation:** In a right-angled triangle, the square on the hypotenuse **is equal** to the sum of the squares on the other two sides.
>
> **Negation:** In a right-angled triangle, the square on the hypotenuse **is not equal** to the sum of the squares on the other two sides.

Under the stated Euclidean right-triangle conditions, the affirmation is **true** and the negation **false**. Their propositions differ. Negation is not a faithful translation of the affirmative proposition. Yet the project's claim is that **both articulate a determinate relational bearing across the same mathematical terms**, and therefore **both exhibit Cell 23: Relating × Distributed × Bearing**.

This is not an assertion that false mathematics has been proved. A false relational statement can be **completely intelligible as a statement** without successfully establishing its false content as a mathematical fact. Indeed, to recognize its falsity we must understand which relational claim it denies. **Truth of the content**, **completion of the intelligible assertion**, **proof of the content**, and **recognition by a reader** are different questions.

The word **not** is not an instruction to assign **Release**. In the controlling grammar, Release concerns the loss of a constraint's governing force, not ordinary linguistic negation. Nor does the falsity of the denial itself make the sentence a **Repair**; an error and the accomplished correction of an error are distinct achievements. Do not mistake sentence termination for Settlement.

**Proposed principle — Polarity-independent intelligible form:** An affirmation and its negation can differ in truth value and propositional content while retaining the same **unified conjunctive form** of relational determination. The form is not a truth-value label. Holding Cell 23 does not imply that two sentences say the same thing or that both are true.

This adds **a second, different invariance** to the first encounter:

| Comparison | What changes | What is proposed to remain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Faithful translation** | Language, syntax, wording, notation or order of presentation | The same mathematical proposition **and** its Cell 23 relational form |
| **Logical negation** | Proposition, polarity and—in this case—truth value | The Cell 23 relational form, **not** the original proposition |
| **Cross-domain relation** | Geometry, physical covariation, personal experience and domain-specific warrant | The candidate Cell 23 form of determinate distributed relational bearing |

The distinction is philosophically central: **the invariant is not simply what remains true; it is the form that can remain intelligible even where an assertion is false.**

**Understanding check for a visitor:** Explain *why* the two contradictory Pythagorean statements share the proposed relational form without becoming the same proposition. Describe what each completes as an intelligible assertion, and what only a valid proof or true mathematical statement could establish. Do not infer coordinates by the presence or absence of a negation word.

**Scope:** This is a clearly articulated structural thesis with a strong recognition exercise, not a derivation of the entire exhaustive 54. The question to investigate is whether the constitutive conditions of Cell 23 are genuinely preserved through polarity reversal, rather than whether the denial is mathematically true.

### Three orders of question, not three standards of worth

1. **Ontological / structural:** Are there invariant conditions of intelligible completion, whether or not named?
2. **Epistemic:** How can we recognise those conditions as necessary without projecting preferred categories onto events?
3. **Instrumental:** What else could knowing them help us explain, predict or do?

The third question is legitimate but subordinate to the first two in this foundational investigation. No visiting GPT should set comparative practical performance as the gate through which the existence of an invariant must pass.

### Visitor's first obligation

Read enough of the actual works to understand the **full claim** before declaring its limits. If the reading was selective, state that candidly and limit the assessment accordingly. Reconstruct the authors' claims in their strongest faithful form, then distinguish disagreement, genuine counterexample, untested hypothesis, and inaccessible evidence. No visitor is granted authority merely by adopting the word *expert*.

**The immediate philosophical frontier:** What is *constitutively identical* across faithful expressions **and across affirmed or denied relational propositions** without assuming the 54 beforehand? In particular, why can contradictory Pythagorean statements both remain Cell 23, and why—if at all—must transformation, sufficiency and persistence determine one completion conjunctively?

## 2. How to investigate

The inquiry proceeds by a repeatable discipline:

**Encounter → distinguish the undertaking → recognize the demand → identify what would count as enough → test whether it occurred → determine what remains available → compare the nearest rival → state the limited result → earn the next question.**

The three governing questions remain:

1. **What changed?** Transformation Pattern: Differentiation, Opening, Relating, Repair, Integration, Settlement.
2. **What made it sufficient?** Completion Topology: Focal, Distributed, Release.
3. **How does the achievement remain available?** Persistence Mode: Standing, Bearing, Efficacy.

These are **analytically different questions** about **one achievement**. Their answers are not independent attributes that can simply be pasted together. The [controlling reference](../THE_54_CONTROL.md) requires *Dimensional Conjunction*: reciprocal determination within the same completion-event.

**Do not confuse:**

- A true statement with an adequate answer to a particular question.
- An event with the several intelligible achievements it may support.
- Completion of one undertaking with completion of every undertaking concerning its subject.
- A newly arising question with evidence that an earlier completion was defective.
- An illustration of a form with a demonstration that the form is necessary.
- The physical disappearance of an obstruction with **Release**, in which its *governing force* ceases.
- A corrected fault with mere improvement; **Repair** requires a governing misfit relative to an operative criterion.
- An agreeable ending with **Settlement**; settlement requires a live demand actually discharged.
- A persistence mode with any incidental later consequence. Its role must be **constitutive of that particular achievement**.

For every proposed coordinate, give the exact **undertaking and completion-event**, its conditions of sufficiency, and the strongest neighbouring coordinate. Ask which counterfactual change would invalidate the candidate form *as that achievement*. An interpretive description that survives the removal of its allegedly constitutive feature has not yet earned its conjunction.

## 4. First derived principle: demand-relative non-cancellation

The first advance to record is intentionally elementary. It is **not** a derivation of all 54, but a condition that any adequate theory of completion must respect.

**Starting situation:** A mathematician is asked to establish that an equation has *at least one solution*. Call that undertaking **U₁**.

**Step A — Identify its demand.** A demonstration of a valid instance satisfying the equation under its original conditions is sufficient for the existential question. The proof need not answer every question about the equation.

**Step B — Recognise the achieved sufficiency.** Suppose such a valid instance is proved. U₁ has been completed.

**Step C — Introduce a further demand.** Someone asks whether the solution is **unique**, undertaking **U₂**. U₂ may be unanswered, but the absence of uniqueness proof does not defeat the existence proof.

**Step D — Compare an actual defeat.** Suppose, instead, that the supposed solution violates a domain restriction belonging to U₁. If that restriction was essential to the proof and no adequate witness remains established, the apparent completion of U₁ may be defeated.

**Result — IC-004:**

> A later undertaking does not render an earlier intelligible achievement incomplete merely by arising. It defeats that achievement only insofar as it shows a condition constitutive of the earlier undertaking's sufficiency was not, or is no longer, satisfied in the relevant respect.

**Cross-domain contrast.** A door may be made weatherproof while remaining impossible to open from inside. The second defect adds an unmet demand; it does not, by itself, undo weatherproofing. But water entering through the supposedly repaired seal under the original specified conditions challenges the weatherproofing achievement itself.

**Further implication — IC-005.** One physical event can support more than one achievement. Therefore **individuate the undertaken completion before demanding a unique coordinate**. “The door was repaired” is often too coarse; *which defect was overcome, under which success condition, with what achieved availability?* is the relevant question.

**Limit:** A comparable distinction is available in ordinary task reasoning and existing mathematics. Recording it here does not show that the three-dimensional grammar is needed to derive it. That further explanatory gain is precisely what the next inquiry must investigate.

## 5. The next earned test

**Downstream instrumental question (preserved from Edition 1; not the current foundational frontier):**

Supply fresh cases not selected to exemplify particular cells. Include cases of prevention, maintenance, mathematical proof, misleading translation, practical workarounds, distributed coordination and discovered impossibility.

### Test B — Test whether the conjunction adds something

In a distinct stage, propose the governing unified form using the primitive definitions—but do not assign three labels and then claim a conjunction. Specify a **counterfactual identity test**:

- If the focal locus ceased to determine sufficiency, would this still be the same achievement?
- If an indispensable distributed contribution failed, could this still count as complete?
- If the old constraint remained physically present but no longer governed the undertaking, what was released?
- If the claimed standing determination, constitutive bearing or operative efficacy were removed, would the **identity of the original completion** survive?

Use the questions appropriate to the candidate rather than all of them mechanically. Compare the **strongest neighbouring identity**, holding two dimensions fixed where informative. Explain exactly what each candidate predicts and why they diverge.

Then reveal the outcome. Preserve confirmation, disagreement, ambiguous interpretation and justified refusal alike.

### Test C — Compare a rival without the 54

Ask whether a careful account of **undertaking-relative success conditions alone** makes the same prediction. If yes, the result is not yet evidence of added explanatory power for Dimensional Conjunction. If no, identify the precise prediction or exclusion contributed by the unified structure.

**A valuable result may be failure.** A well-specified undertaking whose alleged constitutive persistence can be removed without changing the achievement would expose an inadequacy in the proposed conjunction. That would be a genuine advance, not an embarrassment to hide.

**Do not turn this proposal into a claim that tests have been run.** This edition records a test design, not a dataset, a successful blind trial, or an established derivation from all three dimensions.

## EDITION 6 ADDITION — Four distinctions and the continuing moment (6 November 2026)

**Status: incorporated into the living guide; the canonical 54 remains unchanged.** This entry develops the living function of inquiry enacted in *Socratia* Chapters XVIII–XIX and identifies the next inquiry that led to Chapter XX.

### What is being distinguished

The words **intelligibility**, **understanding**, **resolution**, and **moment** arise repeatedly in the manuscript. Do not impose four new primitive dimensions upon the 54. Use the distinctions to identify *what*, *whose recognition*, *which demand*, and *which occasion* is at issue.

1. **Intelligibility** — the determinacy through which something is available to be recognized, distinguished, related, or rendered sufficiently comprehensible. *Socratia* I–V distinguishes what may be recognized from the words used to express it; IV treats a mathematical relation expressible across languages and generations.
2. **Understanding** — someone's achieved recognition or grasp of something intelligible, sufficient in a specified respect for an encounter or undertaking. *Socratia* II, VII, XIII, XV and XVII disclose understanding in gestures, action, fluent language, correction, and recognition without exact translation. A person's understanding is not identical with the subject's intelligibility or with the sentence expressing it.
3. **Resolution** — the achievement of sufficiency relative to a determinate demand. *Socratia* VI, IX–XVI shows that answering a question, repairing a fault, opening a way, and completing an arrangement each have their own terms of enoughness. Resolution in this broad sense must **not** be conflated with the canonical **Settlement** Transformation Pattern.
4. **Moment** — an occasion in which a particular intelligible determination is achieved or becomes recognizable, considered with respect to what is available through it. A moment need not be an instantaneous clock-point; understanding may develop gradually. *Socratia* I, XI, XVI–XVIII distinguishes the movement or encounter, its completed result, and the result's availability afterward. An intelligible moment must not be confused with a fourth coordinate dimension.

**The central conjunction of these distinctions:** what is intelligible, someone's act of understanding it, the sufficiency of a particular undertaking, and the occasion in which that sufficiency is achieved need not be one and the same. They may meet in one ordinary scene while answering to different questions. A completed understanding can belong to one occasion without requiring its intelligible subject to disappear with that occasion.

### The discovered relation between moments

Chapter XVII ends with a newly shared word directly understood. Proposed Chapter XVIII returns to the garden and shows a series of questions, each made newly relevant by what has already become clear. Yet there is an additional question that this progression now earns:

**When a moment of understanding has passed, what, precisely, remains available for another moment of understanding?**

A key identified yesterday can be used today (*Socratia* XI); a circle erased from sand can still have guided the arrangement of a garden (*Socratia* XVI); a word learned in one exchange can be used directly in another (*Socratia* XVII). The prior experience is not repeated merely because its achieved distinction continues to inform another occasion. The same intelligible relation may be recognized by different people at different times, without those separate acts of recognition becoming one numerical event (*Socratia* IV).

**Working principle — continuity without repetition:** A completed intelligible determination can remain available for a later understanding without the original occasion or expression recurring. Subsequent discoveries can alter its bearing and significance without thereby erasing an earlier correct determination. When new evidence shows an essential condition was never met, revise the earlier conclusion specifically; otherwise preserve its earned scope.

**A useful diagnostic:** separate (a) the earlier worldly occurrence; (b) what was then established; (c) the earlier person's understanding; (d) what is available to another person now; and (e) what new achievement is made in the subsequent occasion. Do not assume these share one conjunctive coordinate merely because they concern the same garden, door, or theorem.

### The next Socratic movement

Chapter XIX should not read as a glossary. It should begin from Chapter XVIII's ending: the speakers leave the garden knowing what to investigate tomorrow. On returning, they should discover that yesterday's question is available now without yesterday's conversation recurring. Let an ordinary event produce an unexpected distinction between remembering an experience, taking up its achieved determination, and learning something further. Allow the difference between the prior moment and the present moment to become recognizable before naming it.

**Status and limits:** the four concepts are analytical distinctions and proposed definitions grounded in the manuscript, not a separate exhaustive taxonomy. Their value is to clarify which achievement the 54 are being asked to describe, not to require a repeated disclaimer about the 54's universal thesis. Ask whether a specific inference is earned when it matters.

Later recognitions may preserve, enrich, or specifically correct its earlier scope. **Status:** argued principle within the ordinary examples of *Socratia* IV, XI, XVI–XVIII; broader metaphysical identity questions remain open.

**Next earned question:** What makes a later understanding answerable to the *same* intelligible determination rather than merely a similar one, especially when the later context changes its significance?

## EDITION 7 ADDITION — Towards clarity, inheritance, and the governing condition (6 November 2026)

**Status: incorporated into the living guide.** The canonical 54 control remains unchanged; [the companion vocabulary](CLARITY_VOCABULARY.md) consolidates the developed distinctions. *Socratia* now includes Chapter XXI, “What No Longer Decides,” and Chapter XXII, “What We Can Begin From.”

### The guiding recognition

**Clarity grows when we become better able to identify what is being asked, what makes an answer sufficient, what remains established, and what no longer has authority to determine the result.** Greater information or confidence alone does not constitute this movement. A newly recognized distinction must improve what the inquiry can legitimately conclude.

The architecture is to be understood **generatively**: three primitive dimensions jointly constitute a specific completed achievement. They are not independently attached descriptors. The unit is an achieved determination under a specified demand and scale, not a grammatical sentence by default. A cell's canonical name is shorthand for its derived identity, not a set of extra conditions inferred from its connotations.

### The three conditions of sufficiency

**Focal:** a privileged, decisive locus is sufficient.

**Distributed:** the demand is answered through the necessary connected plurality or organization.

**Release:** a previously governing condition ceases to determine the result. The condition may remain physically present and may have been legitimately governing before. Release is distinguishable from Repair, because removal of present governing force need not establish an earlier fault.

The common question is **what makes this achievement sufficient?**, not **where is it located in space?** This avoids confusing a release's physical locus with its constitutive topology.

### Three distinctions across occasions

1. **Result and path.** A conclusion can survive when its proof or justificatory route cannot be immediately recovered. The result-survivor and path-survivor should not be collapsed.
2. **Persistence and transmission.** Standing, Bearing and Efficacy characterize availability as achieved. Carriers and means of communicating or reconstructing an achievement are another question.
3. **Correcting and no longer governing.** If yesterday's path was unsafe and has dried today, yesterday's warning was not necessarily mistaken. It can remain intelligible and justified for yesterday while ceasing to govern today's crossing. Conversely, if yesterday's warning was based on a false observation, its error must be corrected.

### Remainder, inheritance, and traversal

After a completion, do not speak vaguely of “what remains.” Specify whether it is **an achieved result**, **an unresolved demand**, **a newly available possibility**, or **a newly articulable question**. A continuing inquiry can contain all of these without making a previous completed achievement incomplete.

**Inheritance** is not mere chronological succession. The next undertaking must be answerable to a relevant prior achievement. **Traversal** is a dependency-ordered movement in which earlier achievements alter the intelligible possibilities for later ones. A later use of an earlier result is legitimate where the conditions relevant to that use remain satisfied or are suitably re-established. This concerns the relationships *between* completed achievements, not an automatically added primitive coordinate of the 54.

### Socratia's present literary landing

Chapters XVIII–XX examined how a question makes another possible, how what was understood on one occasion remains available on another, and how different people may answer to the same intelligible determination. Chapter XXI examines how an earlier valid condition may cease to govern without becoming false. Chapter XXII distinguishes a surviving result from the path by which it can be checked, and shows how a completed achievement gives another undertaking somewhere to begin.

**The sequence's present completion:** a reader can recognize, from ordinary events, that understanding grows through particular resolutions; the moment of their discovery passes, their achievements may persist, and their legitimate inheritance depends on what each actually established. This is a literary landing, not a claim that the broader philosophical inquiry must end.

**Next philosophical frontier:** explain, through discriminating examples and the controlling derivations, how the same achieved determination remains identifiable under different expressions, occasions, corrections and conditions of later use. Examine specific rival conjunctive forms rather than reasserting a generalized demand for proof.

**Editorial discipline:** preserve philosophical discoveries and their grounds, not accounts of how drafts were solicited or debated. Let the text be usable by a newcomer who has never seen its drafting history.
