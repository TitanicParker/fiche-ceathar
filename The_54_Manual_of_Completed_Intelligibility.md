# How to use this manual

You do not need to learn a new language before you can use these forms. You already recognise a difference, notice a way through, connect things, put something right, bring parts together and know when a matter is settled. The manual makes those recognitions easier to see and discuss.

Read Part I for the ordinary idea. Part II gives the full definitions. Part III introduces the nine recurring manners of completion. Part IV walks all fifty-four forms. The appendices gather the reference tables, X–Y expressions, utterances and the distinction and derivation rules in one place.

## One small example

Someone says, “That is the correct key.” A difference has become determinate. Someone else says, “It fits this lock.” The key is now understood through a constitutive relationship. Another says, “The door opens.” The key has made something happen. These may concern the same objects, but what became complete is different.

## The scale of the claim

This is a proposed grammar for forms of completed intelligibility. It does not say there are only fifty-four things to think, fifty-four sentences to speak, or fifty-four events in the world. It says a finite set of structural constraints may describe indefinitely many completed achievements. The current written specifications have been tested for pairwise distinction; that is not the same as proving universal completeness or infallible classification.

# Part I — What becomes complete

## Before the answer

A situation can be unfinished in different ways. Two things have not yet been told apart. A route is blocked. A connection has not been made. Something is faulty. Pieces have not yet become a whole. A question is still open. Each unfinished situation calls for a different kind of answer.

## The change, the completion and what remains

The six transformations describe the kind of difference made to an unfinished situation. The nine modes describe a single recognisable manner in which that change is sufficient and remains available. Together they form one completed intelligibility. They are not steps performed one after another.

## What “invariant” means here

Do not imagine a cell as a tiny object with one fixed centre and one perfect sentence describing it. Think of its invariant as a perimeter: the constraints distinguishing what belongs to that form from what does not. Its name and its example are useful points within that range. Other ordinary situations can meet the same constraints without looking alike.

## Finding the operative centre

Start by asking what question or demand has actually been answered. A single afternoon can include a found distinction, a new route, a repaired object and a settled plan. There is no need to force the entire afternoon into one cell. Identify the completed achievement you are talking about. Then ask how it became sufficient and how the achievement remains available.

## No calligraphy required

The forms can be used in ordinary speech. “This one.” “Another way through.” “They fit together.” “That is settled.” The fuller language helps us inspect the pattern; it need not burden everyday recognition.

# Part II — The complete definitions

## The three dimensions

| Dimension              | Controlling definition                                                                        |
|------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Transformation Pattern | Specifies what kind of unresolvedness is altered between the opening and completed situation. |
| Completion Topology    | Specifies the structural shape by which the transformation counts as complete.                |
| Persistence Mode       | Specifies how the achieved completion remains available for subsequent uptake.                |

## Six Transformation Patterns

**Differentiation.** A difference that was unavailable, flattened, conflated, or non-operative becomes determinate enough to matter.

In everyday language: A difference becomes clear enough to matter.

**Opening.** Availability, reach, continuation, access, or possibility becomes greater than it was under the opening constraint.

In everyday language: Something becomes more available.

**Relating.** A bearing, dependency, correspondence, ordering, reciprocity, or mutual determination becomes constitutive of the situation.

In everyday language: A connection becomes part of what the situation is.

**Repair.** An organization that fails relative to an operative criterion is changed so that the misfit or fault no longer governs the result.

In everyday language: A fault stops governing the result.

**Integration.** Separate parts acquire determinate roles through organization into a whole and thereby gain structure, mutual support, or joint capacity.

In everyday language: Parts become a whole with roles.

**Settlement.** A live demand for further uptake, choice, explanation, completion, adjudication, or continuation is discharged.

In everyday language: A live demand for something further is discharged.

## Three Completion Topologies

**Focal.** The transformation becomes complete because completion gathers around one privileged locus. That locus is constitutive of why the transformation counts as complete.

In everyday language: One decisive place or reference makes the result sufficient.

**Distributed.** The transformation becomes complete only across a constitutive plurality, span, sequence, range, reciprocal field, set of cases, or system. No single locus is sufficient by itself.

In everyday language: The full range or set is needed; one point will not do.

**Release.** The transformation becomes complete because something that previously governed the situation loses its determining force. Release means de-governance, not merely removal, absence, negation, separation, physical movement away, or negative wording.

In everyday language: Something that used to decide the matter no longer decides it.

## Three Persistence Modes

**Standing.** The achieved determination itself remains available as the completed result.

In everyday language: What has been settled stands.

**Bearing.** The achieved result remains what it is only through a constitutive relation, dependency, contrast, condition, source, proportion, frame, support, or mutual determination.

In everyday language: What has been settled holds through a relationship.

**Efficacy.** The achieved result remains as an accomplished capacity, operative effect, changed continuation, rule, function, or consequential difference in what can happen.

In everyday language: What has been settled now works or changes what can happen.

## Dimensional Conjunction

The three dimensions determine one completed achievement together. They are not detachable properties of an already completed thing. Focal does not merely pick one example. Distributed does not merely multiply things. Release does not undo the transformation. Standing does not tack on storage; Bearing does not tack on a relationship; Efficacy does not tack on later usefulness.

The notation 6 × 3 × 3 = 54 indexes the possibilities. It does not prescribe a sequence. The alternative 6 × 9 = 54 makes nine recurring Topology × Persistence characters available by name, without replacing the three original dimensions.

# Part III — Nine familiar manners of completion

Each of the nine is a unified Topology × Persistence conjunction, not a fourth primitive or a new thing added to the grammar. These working names are chosen for ease of recognition. Their definitions, not their metaphors, carry the distinction.

| \#  | Mode          | Conjunction            | Plain-language character                                                            |
|-----|---------------|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1   | Anchor        | Focal × Standing       | One decisive place of determination makes the result stand.                         |
| 2   | Pivot         | Focal × Bearing        | One decisive relational point makes the result hold as it does.                     |
| 3   | Actuation     | Focal × Efficacy       | An operative difference is achieved at one decisive point.                          |
| 4   | Configuration | Distributed × Standing | A necessary plurality stands in a determinate arrangement.                          |
| 5   | Nexus         | Distributed × Bearing  | A necessary plurality holds through constitutive relationships.                     |
| 6   | Deployment    | Distributed × Efficacy | An operative achievement needs a span, sequence, field or plurality.                |
| 7   | Independence  | Release × Standing     | A result stands without a previous condition continuing to govern it.               |
| 8   | Regrounding   | Release × Bearing      | A result holds through a positive relationship after former governance loses force. |
| 9   | Displacement  | Release × Efficacy     | A previous governor loses its power to determine what can happen next.              |

## Three ways of looking across each row

Focal: something stands at a decisive locus (Anchor), holds through a decisive relationship (Pivot), or becomes operative there (Actuation). Distributed: a necessary arrangement stands (Configuration), necessary bearings hold it (Nexus), or its necessary span accomplishes something (Deployment). Release: a determination stands beyond former governance (Independence), a positive relationship holds after former governance loses force (Regrounding), or former governance loses operative authority in what now happens (Displacement). These are simultaneous characterisations of completion, not a chronological sequence.

## Names are handles, not centres

“Anchor” is not necessarily a physical anchor, “Pivot” is not necessarily motion, “Deployment” is not a rollout after completion, and “Independence” is not freedom from everything. “Regrounding” does not promise a morally better basis. “Displacement” need not involve movement or replacement. A name is doing its job when it points to the right structural perimeter and lets many ordinary examples fit inside it.

## Nine questions you can actually ask

1\. Anchor: What decisive determination now stands?

2\. Pivot: What decisive relationship now holds the result?

3\. Actuation: What operative change is accomplished at the decisive point?

4\. Configuration: What necessary arrangement now stands?

5\. Nexus: What necessary relationships hold the result?

6\. Deployment: What takes effect across the necessary range?

7\. Independence: What stands without the old governor?

8\. Regrounding: Through what positive bearing does the result now hold?

9\. Displacement: What former control no longer determines what happens?

# Part IV — Walk the fifty-four

The next pages are the heart of the manual. Each entry gives the canonical crystallised name, the original X–Y expression, the minimal companion utterance, the complete ruling resolution from the reference table, and an everyday way to encounter it. “Resolution” says what counts as completion. # Family 1 — Differentiation \| Cells 01–09

A difference becomes clear enough to matter. A distinction is missing, blurred or not yet usable.

### 01 Focal mark

*Differentiation × Anchor (Focal × Standing)*

**X–Y:** X is simply placed as Y.

**Say it:** “This.”

**What completes:** An unresolved particular becomes distinctly available as what it is. The completion resides in the identified particular, rather than in its comparative standing or the response it elicits.

**In ordinary life:** The missing key is identified: “This one.” The achievement is recognising the particular, not yet comparing keys or using it.

### 02 Binary bearing

*Differentiation × Pivot (Focal × Bearing)*

**X–Y:** X is classified as one of Y₁ / Y₂.

**Say it:** “This, not that.”

**What completes:** A particular becomes distinctly exceptional, prominent, or otherwise set apart through a constitutive contrast. Its achieved distinctness is its situated standing, not simply its independently identified identity.

**In ordinary life:** Two similar cups are sorted into “mine” and “yours.” The distinction holds through the contrast.

### 03 Boundary act

*Differentiation × Actuation (Focal × Efficacy)*

**X–Y:** X is separated from Y by a boundary.

**Say it:** “This far.”

**What completes:** A decisive difference becomes an operative discriminator. Completion consists in the distinction determining which continuation occurs at a privileged threshold or decision point.

**In ordinary life:** An age check determines who can enter. The boundary is not merely noticed: it decides what happens.

### 04 Scope standing

*Differentiation × Configuration (Distributed × Standing)*

**X–Y:** X is constrained to the specific Y.

**Say it:** “Within this.”

**What completes:** A previously conflated range becomes an articulated pattern of differences. The achieved result is the determinate differentiation of the relevant field, not an isolated identified member.

**In ordinary life:** A shelf of mixed tools is organised into recognisable kinds. The distinctions stand across the shelf.

### 05 Context-bound mark

*Differentiation × Nexus (Distributed × Bearing)*

**X–Y:** X indicates absent Y.

**Say it:** “This here.”

**What completes:** A field becomes differentiated through the contextual standings of its members. What distinguishes a member is constituted by the company, scale, or environment in which it stands.

**In ordinary life:** On a map, a tiny dot matters because of where it sits among roads and towns.

### 06 Field separation

*Differentiation × Deployment (Distributed × Efficacy)*

**X–Y:** X differs from Y only within scope S.

**Say it:** “These among these.”

**What completes:** A range of relevant differences becomes a differentiated practice of response. Its completion is an achieved discrimination across cases that can no longer be handled interchangeably.

**In ordinary life:** A sorting system treats glass, paper and food waste differently across the collection route.

### 07 Opposed standing

*Differentiation × Independence (Release × Standing)*

**X–Y:** X stands against Y.

**Say it:** “Opposed.”

**What completes:** A definite difference becomes available when an imposed sameness or categorisation ceases to determine what can be distinguished. The achieved distinction stands beyond the formerly governing conflation.

**In ordinary life:** Two views once dismissed as “the same complaint” are allowed to stand as genuinely opposed positions.

### 08 Graded contrast

*Differentiation × Regrounding (Release × Bearing)*

**X–Y:** X varies toward Y along a continuum.

**Say it:** “More and less.”

**What completes:** Difference becomes determinate through comparison after a privileged norm ceases to dictate its interpretation. The achieved distinction is comparative without one term having to count as the standard from which the others deviate.

**In ordinary life:** Colours can be ordered from lighter to darker without one being the “correct” colour.

### 09 Exclusive cut

*Differentiation × Displacement (Release × Efficacy)*

**X–Y:** X selects only one among Y options.

**Say it:** “Not admitted.”

**What completes:** An inherited conflation loses authority over response, allowing relevant differences to govern selection or exclusion. The completion is discrimination freed from a classification that previously overrode it.

**In ordinary life:** At a gate, a valid membership check excludes entries that an old catch-all category would have admitted.

# Family 4 — Repair \| Cells 28–36

**Say it:** “Settled if.”

**What completes:** A field of claims becomes settled through the conditions and recognitions that constitute closure across it. The achieved settlement is a supported pattern of closure, not merely a list of final answers.

**In ordinary life:** All cases are settled by a shared rule that states which conditions must hold.

### 51 Case settlement

*Settlement × Deployment (Distributed × Efficacy)*

**X–Y:** X typically produces Y across cases.

**Say it:** “Settled across the cases.”

**What completes:** Unresolved demands across an undertaking are discharged as an executable course of continuation. The achievement is a settled practice or procedure that does not leave the requisite next moves undecided.

**In ordinary life:** A service adopts a procedure that resolves the remaining steps across the cases it handles.

### 52 Transformation release

*Settlement × Independence (Release × Standing)*

**X–Y:** X remains Y under all transformations.

**Say it:** “Still itself.”

**What completes:** Definite closure becomes available when a remainder ceases to require further adjudication. What persists is the settled determination, despite objections, variation, or incompleteness that no longer governs whether the matter must remain open.

**In ordinary life:** A melody remains recognised as itself after transposition; the former key need not settle its identity.

### 53 Retrospective settlement

*Settlement × Regrounding (Release × Bearing)*

**X–Y:** X reinterprets earlier states as Y.

**Say it:** “Now it makes sense.”

**What completes:** A demand for further explanation, accounting, or redress loses force through a constitutive reconnection. Closure exists through the bearing that makes the remaining material intelligible or the former claim discharged.

**In ordinary life:** Earlier unusual readings make sense once their connection to a hidden fault is discovered.

### 54 Continuation discharge

*Settlement × Displacement (Release × Efficacy)*

**X–Y:** X settles completely into Y.

**Say it:** “Nothing further is owed.”

**What completes:** Continuation becomes free of a demand that had required return, repetition, or further uptake. Settlement is accomplished as the ability to proceed without that unfinished matter governing the next move.

**In ordinary life:** The closing act of a ceremony is completed; no further rite is required.

# Part V — Learning to recognise the form

## Start with the unfinished question

A description of a bicycle does not tell you what has become complete. If the problem is that two bolts are mixed up, the achievement may be Differentiation. If the route is blocked, it may be Opening. If a wheel wobbles against its criterion of fit, Repair becomes relevant. The topic alone never gives the coordinate.

## Then see what makes this answer enough

Is there a decisive point? A necessary span? Has an earlier condition stopped deciding the result? These are different ways a transformation can reach sufficiency. A repair with many screws is not automatically Distributed; if one decisive adjustment constitutes its completion, it may be Focal.

## Then see what remains

Can the result now be pointed to as settled? Does it hold only through a continuing relation? Is the achievement an operative capacity or consequence? This is not a question about whether anything lasts forever. It is a question about the form in which the completion remains available.

## One situation, several completions

A door is identified, its hinges adjusted, and it swings freely. Those can be three distinct achievements: locating a particular, correcting a misfit, and gaining an operative capacity. The grammar follows completed intelligibilities, not whole episodes treated as indivisible objects.

## What the pairwise work settles

The existing written specification reports a distinction for all 1,431 unordered cell pairs. It also distinguishes that structural finding from three further questions: whether a sentence can be positively recovered, whether it receives exactly one placement, and whether the whole field is universally exhaustive. The manual preserves that distinction rather than making a larger claim.

# Appendix C — Numbering and retrieval

Write the transformations in the order Differentiation, Opening, Relating, Repair, Integration, Settlement. Within each family, write Focal, Distributed, Release. Within each topology, write Standing, Bearing, Efficacy.

The numbering is the same in every family: Anchor is first, Pivot second, Actuation third; Configuration fourth, Nexus fifth, Deployment sixth; Independence seventh, Regrounding eighth, Displacement ninth.

With zero-based indices for transformation (i), topology (j) and persistence (k), the number is 9i + 3j + k + 1. This is an address rule; it is not a claim that a cell is built in three successive stages.

## The positive recovery procedure

• Read the situation in its ordinary context.

• Ask exactly what was unfinished.

• Identify the answering transformation.

• Identify what made the result sufficient.

• Identify what now remains available.

• Recover the full coordinate and compare it with the complete ruling characterisation.

• Keep the classification open if the completed achievement itself is not yet specified.

## How to use a neighbouring distinction

Compare forms only after identifying the positive achievement. If both descriptions seem plausible, ask which fact is constitutive rather than merely present. Does a plurality happen to be involved, or is its whole range necessary? Does a former rule happen to disappear, or is its loss what makes the result complete? Is an effect merely a later consequence, or is it the accomplished result itself?

# Appendix D — The perimeter rule and the claim of distinctness

The forms are not particles or miniature objects. They are specified manners in which something becomes intelligibly complete. A cell’s invariant identity can be understood as its perimeter: the constitutive constraints that its legitimate realisations must satisfy. No unique sentence or example is required to sit at the centre.

The 54 crystallised names are compact, useful reference handles. The companion utterances bring the achievement closer to ordinary speech. The X–Y expressions record an earlier relational view of the movement. The ruling resolutions state the complete structural character more precisely. None of these alone exhausts everything that may occur within a form.

The earlier pairwise distinction register found all 1,431 unordered cell-pairs distinct in the current written specification, with cell names unnecessary to those distinctions. That does not by itself establish exact-one classification of every utterance, universal completeness, or universal psychological recognition. The different claims should remain separate.
