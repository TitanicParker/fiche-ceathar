## What the documents show

The present Fiche Ceathar method begins its reading with a sentence, finds the independently established Operative Centre, and examines the one three-dimensional structural conjunction that expresses its achieved intelligibility. The three early documents show that the history of the inquiry is **larger than its present reading method**.

The *Provenance* narrative describes a movement from geometry and perception, through relational dynamics and iterative computation, into repeated patterns and a 54-form vocabulary, and only subsequently into sentences. *People Speak* describes an earlier sentence-oriented account of X → Y transformations and six families, before the current canonical dimensions were fully distinguished. *Connie Conscious* treats the sentence as shareable, stabilised awareness and develops the idea that the structural settling of meaning is usually invisible during fluent understanding.

The oldest layer is thus neither an ontology inferred from sentences nor merely a poetic afterthought attached to the 54. It is an earlier geometric and philosophical line of inquiry that eventually found a usable sentence instrument.

## The Pythagorean Gate: the actual mathematical starting point described

The provenance text describes inspecting an arrangement of equal-radius circles, superimposing Cartesian coordinates and becoming interested in the difference between orthogonal axes and directions separated by 60 degrees.

For two Euclidean vectors of lengths *b* and *c* at angle *A*, the cosine rule gives

`a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos(A)`.

At a right angle, `cos(A) = 0`, leaving the Pythagorean relation. In a 60-degree basis, if the squared vector is `|m e₁ + n e₂|²` with unit vectors at 60 degrees, then

`Q(m,n) = m² + n² + mn`.

The cross-term reflects the non-orthogonality of the chosen basis. **This is a real mathematical difference**, and the source narrative identifies its recognition as a formative encounter. The more expansive claim that Cartesian geometry therefore *loses relation*, or that a single relational structure underlies geometry, consciousness and language, is the philosophical interpretation developed from that encounter. The mathematics itself does not force that entire interpretation.

This distinction strengthens the provenance: it allows the first act to be seen with both its exact mathematical content and its subsequent intellectual significance.

## Three early documents, three different voices

### 1. Provenance of the System — a genealogy of the instrument

Its importance is chronological. It tells how an initial geometrical tension became a line of inquiry into local relation, emergence, recurrence, stabilisation and invariant forms. Its later transition from theorems to sentences is explicitly described as a **change of application**, rather than the spontaneous invention of the underlying structure.

The text includes assertions about empirical saturation, fixed-point invariants and deterministic classification. Those are part of the developmental record. The present conjunction discipline must not be reverse-engineered into the early stages as if its terminology had always been there.

### 2. People Speak — the earlier structural vocabulary

The central question is, **what is a sentence doing?** The text models sentences as changes to an understanding, using `X → Y`, and organizes six transformation families. It then introduces three settlement types—State, Relation and Event—and directions—Toward, Across and Away. These form an earlier 3 × 3 account.

It is possible to see the line leading toward the later 54, but the old labels do **not** translate mechanically into Focal / Distributed / Release or Standing / Bearing / Efficacy. The latter are defined by the present controlling reference as jointly determining one completed achievement, and cannot be treated as successive operations added to an already completed transformation.

This is a historical document showing **how the inquiry spoke before the terminology was crystallized**.

### 3. Connie Conscious — the perceptual claim

The opening statement says:

> “We notice what we mean. We do not notice how meaning settles.”

The document explores sentences as acts of awareness that can be made portable across persons and time. It also demonstrates its early cell vocabulary by attempting to classify sentences in its own prose, then reflecting on the recurring “landings” it detects.

This is a record of a recognitional ambition: learn to attend to the form of a meaningful achievement rather than only to its semantic subject matter. The document's particular historical cell classifications are **not automatically ratified** by the later canonical ruling definitions.

## What the Origins entrance adds to the Metaphysical Corner

The Corner previously began with three philosophical portals: priorness to language, constancy of form, and the Pythagorean Gate. The three historical texts now disclose a fourth kind of access: **provenance**.

- **Before language** is not solely a speculative question. Earlier texts already examine perception, relation and pre-verbal intelligibility.
- **Constancy** is not solely an abstraction. The early operational phase sought recurrence and stabilisation across executions, a claim that can be checked against the original logs.
- **The Pythagorean Gate** has two distinct senses. Its philosophical sense asks how a mathematical relation can remain available across people and eras. Its historical-mathematical sense begins with right-angle specialisation and the non-orthogonal cross-term. Both belong in the Corner; they should not be conflated.
